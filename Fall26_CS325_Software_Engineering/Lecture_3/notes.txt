Agile Development
Agile development is a software development approach where software is developed incrementally and iteratively, with continuous customer feedback, collaboration, and adaptation to changing requirements.


Why Agile?

Business and technology change rapidly
Traditional projects give late feedback
Long delivery cycles = high risk
Agile embraces change and delivers value early

Agile Manifesto

2001: Kent Beck and 16 other software developers, referred to as “Agile Alliance”, signed the “manifesto for Agile software development”. 

Individuals and interaction over processes and tool
Working software over comprehensive documentation 
Customer collaboration over contract negotiation 
Responding to change over following plan

That is, while there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more.


Agile principles

1.Highest priority is to satisfy the customer through early and continues delivery of valuable software. 
2.Welcome changing requirements even late in the development (for customers competitive advantage). 
3.Deliver working software frequently, from couple of weeks to couple of months. 
4.Business people and developers must work together daily throughout the project. 
5.Build project around motivated Individuals. Give them the environment and support they need, and trust them to get the job done. 
6.Most efficient and effective method of conveying information to and with team is face-to-face conversation.
7.Working software is the primary measure of progress. 
8.Agile processes promote sustainable development. The sponsors, developers, and users should be able to maintain a constant pace indefinitely. 
9.Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design enhances agility. 
10.Simplicity--the art of maximizing the amount of work not done--is essential. 
11.The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge from self-organizing teams. 
12.At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior accordingly

Plan driven vs Agile development
Aspect	Plan-Driven Development	Agile Development
Approach	Development follows a predefined plan	Development is iterative and incremental
Requirements	Requirements are defined early and changes are discouraged	Requirements can evolve throughout development
Planning	Detailed planning is done at the beginning	Planning is done continuously in short iterations
Development	Usually follows sequential phases	Work is divided into small iterations/sprints
Customer involvement	Mainly at the beginning and end	Frequent and continuous involvement
Changes	Changes can be costly and difficult	Changes are expected and accommodated
Testing	Often performed after major development phases	Testing occurs continuously
Delivery	Usually one major release at the end	Frequent releases/increments
Documentation	Extensive documentation	Documentation is important but generally less emphasized than working software
Best suited for	Stable, well-understood requirements	Changing or uncertain requirements
Risk	Risks may be discovered late	Risks can be identified earlier through iterations
Examples	Waterfall, V-Model	Scrum, Extreme Programming (XP)
