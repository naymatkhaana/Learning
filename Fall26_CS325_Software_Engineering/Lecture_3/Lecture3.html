01 • Foundation

What is Agile?

⚙️

Agile development is a software development approach where software is developed incrementally and iteratively, with continuous customer feedback, collaboration, and adaptation to changing requirements.

02 • Two Mindsets

Plan-Driven vs Agile

📋

Plan-Driven

“Plan first, then build according to the plan.”

The project is planned in detail before development begins. Changes are generally harder to accommodate.

🔄

Agile

“Build in small steps, get feedback, and adapt the plan.”

The team develops incrementally, receives feedback, and continuously adjusts priorities.

03 • Why Agile?

Why do we need Agile?

1

Business & Technology Change

Customer expectations, competitors, and technologies can change rapidly.

2

Late Feedback

Traditional projects may deliver working software only after long development cycles.

3

Long Delivery = High Risk

The longer we wait to deliver, the greater the risk of building the wrong product.

4

Agile Embraces Change

Agile delivers value early and uses feedback to guide future development.

04 • The Agile Manifesto

The Agile Manifesto

📜

In 2001, Kent Beck and 16 other software developers created and signed the Manifesto for Agile Software Development, commonly associated with the Agile Alliance.

06 • Important Clarification

What does "over" mean?

⚖️

“While there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more. ”

Agile does NOT mean “ignore documentation, contracts, plans, or processes.”

It means that when there is a conflict, people, working software, collaboration, and adaptability are given greater priority.

09 • Comparison

Plan-Driven vs Agile

Aspect 📋 Plan-Driven ⚡ Agile
Approach Predefined plan Iterative & incremental
Requirements Defined early Can evolve
Planning Detailed planning at beginning Continuous planning
Development Sequential phases Short iterations / sprints
Customer Mainly beginning/end Frequent involvement
Changes Costly & difficult Expected & accommodated
Testing Often after development phases Continuous
Delivery Usually one major release Frequent releases
Documentation Extensive Important but working software prioritized
Best suited for Stable requirements Changing / uncertain requirements
Risk May be discovered late Identified earlier
Examples Waterfall, V-Model Scrum, XP
Key Takeaway

Don't just follow the plan.
Learn and adapt.

Build → Deliver → Get Feedback → Adapt → Improve
