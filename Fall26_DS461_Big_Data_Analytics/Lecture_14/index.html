Part 01

What is Entity Resolution?

Entity resolution means determining whether records from different datasets refer to the same real-world entity.

Are these the same person?

Look carefully at the information in the two databases.

Database A

Name William S. Jones
Address 24 Main Street
Phone 555-1234

Database B

Name Bill Jones
Address 24 Main St.
Phone 555-9876
What do you think?

Could these two records represent the same person?

Yes, they could be the same person.

Names can differ because of nicknames: William → Bill
Addresses can differ because of formatting: Street → St.
Phone numbers can differ because someone may provide a home phone to one company and a mobile phone to another.

This is why entity resolution requires similarity, not simply exact equality.

Traditional matching

"Are the two values exactly equal?"

William ≠ Bill

This can incorrectly reject real matches.

Entity resolution

"How likely is it that these records describe the same entity?"

William ≈ Bill

We use multiple pieces of evidence to decide.

Part 02

One Trillion Possible Pairs

Now imagine two companies, each with one million customer records.

Company A × Company B

1,000,000 × 1,000,000

Every record from A could potentially be compared with every record from B.

Why is this a problem?

One million records in A multiplied by one million records in B gives:

1,000,000 × 1,000,000 = 1,000,000,000,000

That's one trillion possible pairs.

Comparing every pair is extremely expensive. LSH helps us avoid most of those comparisons.

Fingerprint Matching

A fingerprint can be converted from an image into a set of important locations called minutiae.

What is a minutia?

A minutia is an important point in a fingerprint, such as where a ridge ends or where two ridges merge.

Ridge merging

Turn the fingerprint into a set

Divide the fingerprint into grid squares. A square containing a minutia becomes part of the set.
